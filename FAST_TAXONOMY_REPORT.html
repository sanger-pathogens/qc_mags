01 Why the fork exists

The bottleneck was never compute — it was the size of the request.

GTDB-Tk is the slowest step in qc_mags, and upstream runs it with --place_species. That flag forces every genome through pplacer's reference-tree placement. Placement is expensive and almost entirely fixed-cost: loading and caching the class-level tree runs to roughly 50 GB of likelihood data, and GTDB-Tk repeats it once per class-level tree, five or six times in a typical batch.

The resulting task asks for 8 cores and 64 GB. On a busy farm that combination is close to unschedulable — memory is the scarce resource, not slots. During this work farm22 had 6,592 idle slots but only 17 of ~115 hosts with 32 GB free, and at one point zero hosts could satisfy 64 GB plus 8 free processor units.

The key observation is that --place_species does not decide species assignment. GTDB-Tk's skani ANI screen does that, and where the two disagree the ANI result wins. Placement adds confirmation the final call never uses.

02 The change

Three lines in gtdbtk.nf, one parameter, upstream path untouched.

- label params.batch_size > 100 ? "cpu_64" : "cpu_8"
+ label params.fast_taxonomy ? (params.batch_size > 100 ? "cpu_4"  : "cpu_2")
+                            : (params.batch_size > 100 ? "cpu_64" : "cpu_8")

- label params.temp_file_storage ? "mem_64" : "mem_120"
+ label params.fast_taxonomy ? (params.batch_size > 100 ? "mem_50" : "mem_32")
+                            : (params.temp_file_storage ? "mem_64" : "mem_120")

+ place_flag = params.fast_taxonomy ? "" : "--place_species"
- gtdbtk classify_wf ... --place_species --cpus ${task.cpus} ...
+ gtdbtk classify_wf ... ${place_flag} --cpus ${task.cpus} ...

Both label expressions are gated on fast_taxonomy first, so with the flag false the generated bsub is byte-identical to upstream. The default is true on this branch for team162's human and clinical isolates, and should stay false upstream — see scope.

Two findings drive the label choices. Fast mode's memory is flat across a 4× batch range (38.3 → 36.6 GB), so the ~37 GB is a fixed reference-data load rather than a per-genome cost — batching is effectively free in memory terms. And fast mode uses 1.28–1.36 cores regardless of batch size, which is why the original cpu_64 on the batched branch was 47× the measured need and had to be corrected.

The ANI miss rate held at 0.60–0.87% across 5,000 genomes. That figure is the entire basis of the speed-up, and the reason the default is dataset-dependent.

The saving splits sharply on one variable: whether any genome in the sample misses the ANI screen. If none does, the sample never loads the reference tree and finishes in about a minute — a 46–70× saving. If even one does, the fixed cost is paid anyway and the saving falls to roughly 2×.

Per-sample runs therefore benefit most, since small samples have the least to amortise the tree load against. Memory was identical in both modes at 14.6–14.8 GB, giving mem_32 a 2.2× margin.

Reproducibility was checked separately: three independent fast-mode runs over the same six samples agreed 36/36 with each other, on genome names and pass sets as well as classifications.

Why agreement holds despite internal disagreement

In place mode GTDB-Tk records whether tree topology and ANI concur. They disagreed on 12.9–14.8% of genomes at every batch size — yet the final call never changed, because ANI takes precedence for species assignment. Placement produces a second opinion the pipeline discards.

The hard case

The Bifidobacterium animalis / animalis_B split is a near-threshold discrimination inside one species complex, and it defeated an earlier hand-rolled skani workaround. Across 119 genomes from that complex in the validation set, fast mode partitioned them exactly as place mode did — at 2000 genomes, 34 as animalis_B and 11 as plain animalis.

Of 127 samples, 126 were resolved by the ANI screen alone. A single sample needed pplacer and consumed 27 of the 200 compute-minutes — roughly as much as the other 126 combined. Under upstream settings every sample would have paid that cost, which is precisely why the original run managed 60 samples in 32 hours before stalling with zero schedulable hosts.

07 Scope and caveats

What these numbers do and do not support.

Read this before adopting

One dataset. Every measurement comes from human and clinical isolates where the ANI screen resolves 99.1–100% of genomes. For cohorts rich in novel or under-represented taxa, more genomes fall through to the tree and both the speed-up and the equivalence argument weaken.

The upstream default must stay false. This branch defaults to true for team162 only. The 12.9–14.8% topology/ANI disagreement rate shows the tree genuinely would say something different — it simply loses to ANI on this data.

Wall-clock is queue-dependent. The 36-minute production figure benefited from a near-empty queue. The durable claims are the compute reduction and the scheduling one, not elapsed time.

Timing noise is large. The same sample varied 24.3 vs 34.8 min on identical input — about 40%. Single-run comparisons below ~1.5× are not meaningful here.

08 Rejected and recorded

Two things tested that did not survive contact with measurement.

--pplacer_cpus — no effect

Tested on matched pairs across six samples. pplacer sat at 1.00–1.02 cores for 24–33 minutes whatever the setting: it is strictly single-threaded and the flag cannot change that. Results also crossed over between arms, well inside the noise floor. The branch was retired rather than shipped, so nobody tunes a knob that does nothing.

Nextflow's resource fields are unusable on this container

Nextflow sums peak_rss and %cpu across the process tree. GTDB-Tk forks ~20 processes that memory-map the same reference package, so a task LSF measured at 14.8 GB and 1.0 cores was reported as 190 GB and 988% — a 12.9× overcount. A %mem above 100 is the giveaway. Every label decision here was taken from .command.log instead.
